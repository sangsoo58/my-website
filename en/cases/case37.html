CASE37 · Renewable energy · MATLAB solar-resource analysis

Comparing solar installation conditions with hourly irradiance modeling

Weather data and solar geometry construct horizontal and tilted-surface radiation over 8,760 hours. Tilt, azimuth and available surfaces are compared before downstream PV generation and economic modeling.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Resource before equipment sizing

The analysis begins with site-specific weather and irradiance, including multiple Korean regions, rather than assuming that panel capacity alone determines output.

Latitude, longitude, standard meridian, declination, hour angle, zenith angle and surface incidence determine the hourly geometric relationship.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Tilted-surface radiation model

The source combines beam, diffuse and ground-reflected components: IT = Ib × Rb × η + Id × (1 + cosβ)/2 × η + IH × ρg × (1 − cosβ)/2 × η.

Here β is tilt and ρg ground reflectance. The source coefficient η is a calculation factor; it must not be reinterpreted as a complete PV module/inverter/system loss model.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Hourly-to-annual aggregation

Aggregate hourly results to daily, monthly and annual radiation. Compare installation candidates by annual resource, not just the largest instantaneous value.

The MATLAB sweep evaluates tilts from 0° to 90° in 5° steps, alongside azimuth and roof/wall installation surfaces.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering constraints and scope

Annual radiation maximum is not necessarily the complete installation optimum. Roof geometry, shading, structure, wind loading, maintenance and module packing also matter.

PV capacity, generation, self-consumption, tariffs and payback require separate downstream models. The source does not quantify achieved PV generation or project savings.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Transferable workflow

Select site/weather; compute solar geometry; separate irradiance components; apply surface tilt/azimuth; aggregate results; compare feasible installation conditions.

Keep input provenance and resource units explicit when passing the results to a PV performance model.
