CASE19 · Manufacturing · Chillers, compressors and boilers

Diagnosing integrated utility performance and metering quality in a chemical plant

Representative seasonal records compare cooling, compressed air and steam. Equipment efficiency differences are evaluated alongside abnormal flow measurements before savings priorities are assigned.

English technical edition · Original case and supporting material (Korean) ↗
Field diagnosis

Analyzed utility energy cost, not savings

KRW 19.4 billion/year
Site
Anonymized chemical manufacturer A, Korea
Equipment
Chillers, compressors and boilers
Analysis period
Seven days in each of four representative months of 2008; ten-minute records
Energy
Electricity and gas

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Cooling, compressed air and steam represented approximately KRW 19.4 billion/year. Equipment-level intensity differences could have significant cost consequences, but suspect flow records limited the reliability of some comparisons.

The study covered 28 chillers in three cooling systems, 16 compressors in two air systems and six boilers. Each monitored point had 4,032 records across the four representative weeks.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and performance diagnosis

Chiller power and water temperatures, header pressures/bypass, compressor power/discharge conditions, airflow/pressure/dew point, boiler gas/steam flow, pressure, flue temperature and oxygen were analyzed with weather.

Cooling-system UPI averaged about 0.68/0.49/0.48 kW/RT. Individual chillers ranged from approximately 0.43 to 0.77 kW/RT. Compressor system intensities were approximately 0.217/0.195 in the source, but airflow consistency needed checking. Normal boiler records ranged from 43.9 to 49.6 Nm³/ton of steam.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Improvement priorities

Prioritize efficient chillers and review water temperatures, load and bypass together. Calibrate air and steam flow meters before quantifying compressor or boiler savings.

For compressed air, examine load, intake temperature and discharge pressure after data quality is established. For boilers, combine efficient-unit priority with appropriate pressure and combustion conditions. Cost shares were cooling 45%, boilers 38% and compressed air 17%.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Evidence and M&V

The approximately 44% difference between better and worse chiller intensities is an equipment-performance spread, not a confirmed savings rate.

The public source does not provide a clear final quantified saving from the report’s simulation section. No substitute total is invented here. Actual improvements require baselines for cooling load, airflow demand, steam production, weather and production conditions.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

A large apparent efficiency difference can be caused by bad denominator data. Correct flow measurement before using intensity benchmarks for operating decisions or financial claims.

Evidence & interpretation

This is an operating diagnosis without a confirmed total savings figure. The source’s general compressed-air management reference of 0.13 or below is not a verified rating for the target equipment and does not override measurement checks.

Source basis: 2008 large chemical facility utility operating-characteristics report.
