CASE04 · Manufacturing · Melting and casting

Increasing tapping throughput and casting batch size to improve energy intensity

Separate gas baselines for two melting furnaces and an electricity baseline for casting machine #8 evaluate the effect of higher daily throughput and larger batches under comparable process conditions.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Melting furnaces #2 and #4 showed different gas intensities. Tapping temperature, waiting and throughput needed to be examined to explain the higher intensity of furnace #4.

Casting machine #8 also showed product-dependent electricity intensity. The analysis examined whether larger batches of the same product and recipe could spread fixed consumption across more output.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and baseline models

MES tapping mass and casting quantity were aligned with EMS gas and electricity data. Product, recipe and batch information defined comparable operating conditions.

For the melting models, Q is daily tapped mass in kg and U(Q) is gas intensity in Nm³/kg. For the casting model, Q is daily output in pieces and U(Q) is kWh per piece. The same symbol therefore has different equipment-specific units.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Proposed operating change

The melting scenarios move throughput from the median (Q2) to the third quartile (Q3). Casting output increases from 690 to 895 pieces/day through larger batches of the same product and recipe.

For furnace #4, the revised baseline indicates approximately 220 Nm³/day of potential at the Q3 output level. The three percentages apply to separate equipment boundaries and should not be added as a site-wide savings rate.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Measurement and verification

Record tapping temperature, waiting and operating conditions while comparing the Q2 and Q3 throughput regimes. For casting machine #8, change batch size while keeping product and recipe comparable.

Evaluate energy intensity and absolute energy separately. Higher production may improve energy per unit while increasing total energy consumption.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

Production planning can change energy intensity by distributing fixed consumption across more output. Equipment-specific baselines make this effect explicit and help separate production growth from operational savings.

Evidence & interpretation

All three improvement rates are model-based scenarios awaiting field verification. Reported rounded values and equipment boundaries are preserved.

Source basis: Manufacturer D M&V plan and revised-baseline savings-potential report.
