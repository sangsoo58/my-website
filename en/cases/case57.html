CASE57 · Manufacturing · Dual-temperature load allocation

Balancing 5°C and 13°C chilled-water loads with chiller and pump staging

Historical demand, valve, ΔT and staging data motivate a temperature-level and load-sharing test. The plan checks OAC and process conditions before deciding how many chillers and pumps should run.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Match temperature level to actual need

Some loads require low-temperature cooling or dehumidification; others may accept a higher-temperature supply. Evaluate each use rather than reducing the 5°C fraction indiscriminately.

Chiller and pump effects must be considered together because changing temperature or load allocation can change required flow.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Historical operating observations

Representative January, April and July periods had around 50% average load, versus approximately 75% in October. A separate annual analysis reported roughly 63% mean load and one or two extra operating units.

An example January demand around 5,000 RT was served by approximately 8,000 RT online capacity. Different analysis windows explain why these load figures should not be combined as one identical record.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Load-sharing and hydraulic clues

Historical average load split was approximately 60% at 5°C and 40% at 13°C, with winter 5°C share above 70%. The observations do not themselves prove that this split was wrong.

The 5°C ΔT was often below the 10°C design value; the 13°C ΔT was often below 5°C. Bypass was open much of the time, averaging around 12%.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

The actual test plan

The project planned a 15–50% closing adjustment of OAC 13°C valves, checking whether the 13°C operating count could fall and then defining management criteria.

Other plan material specified a one-unit 13°C shutdown test with outdoor dew point at or below 22°C. This is a project test condition, not a universal control rule.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Evaluate the complete response

Measure coil leaving/dew-point conditions, cleanroom temperature/humidity, both systems’ RT/flow/ΔT and chiller/pump power after stabilization.

No isolated final saving amount is assigned to this activity. Preserve actual plan language rather than rewriting a planned valve action into a completed successful result.

Evidence & interpretation

This case contains field diagnosis and a documented optimization plan. It does not claim the valve changes or shutdown produced an independently quantified final saving.

Source basis: Dual-temperature chilled-water analysis and OAC optimization test plan.
