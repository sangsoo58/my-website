CASE51 · Buildings · eQUEST / historical Appendix G

Constructing a fair Appendix G baseline for a seventy-two-floor LEED energy model

A real design-modeling project compares a mixed-use proposed building with four rotated historical ASHRAE baselines. The cost result is separated from site-energy use and certification claims.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Project and modeling scope

The 72-floor building combined office and hotel uses with different schedules: offices five days/week, 07:00–20:00; hotels seven days/week, 24 hours/day.

Gross area was 1,594,260 ft², with 1,192,722 ft² conditioned and 401,538 ft² unconditioned. Proposed glazing ratio was approximately 70%.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Build the proposed model and verify inputs

The model included envelope, internal gains, HVAC, process loads, external lighting, parking fans, elevators, tariffs and schedules with hourly TRY weather.

Verification and ES-D, PS-E, BEPS, PS-B and PS-D reports checked input consistency, end uses, cost, peak demand and plant load coverage.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

The baseline is rule-defined

The historical baseline used 40% glazing distributed across four orientations, Climate Zone 4A envelope assumptions, office System 7 VAV with hot-water reheat and hotel System 2 PTHP.

Annual runs at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° were averaged. A baseline is not merely the proposed system with slightly worse equipment efficiency.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Cost result and energy-source boundary

The four baseline costs were approximately USD 3.715, 3.703, 3.710 and 3.757 million/year, averaging USD 3.721 million. Proposed cost was approximately USD 3.574 million, about 4% lower.

The related design comparison reports higher proposed site energy, approximately 139,539 versus 102,348 MBtu/year, due to purchased-heat cooling. Lower cost is not lower site energy.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering interpretation

Calculate percentage improvement as (baseline cost − proposed cost) / baseline cost × 100. Interpret lighting, fans, electric cooling and purchased heat together.

CASE35 describes the related design comparison. This is historical modeling evidence; it is neither a completed operating saving nor a new LEED certification.

Source-value comparisons

Evidence & interpretation

The 4% is a modeled energy-cost improvement under historical Appendix G rules. It is not the overall LEED score, measured savings or a statement of current certification requirements.
