CASE30 · Buildings · Retrofit modeling and LCC

Evaluating what to retain, repair or replace before a major building retrofit

Condition assessment and a DOE-2.1E baseline model support retrofit alternatives for the envelope, lighting, HVAC and plant. Life-cycle cost links initial investment to annual energy cost.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Start with existing equipment condition

The first decision was which assets could remain in service, which required repair and which justified replacement. Efficiency alone did not describe leakage, corrosion, insulation or control condition.

A site checklist organized envelope, air conditioning, heating, cooling, hot-water and electrical-system inspection.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Baseline modeling method

Collect drawings, weather, envelope properties, equipment specifications, schedules, utility use and tariffs. Convert the existing building into a DOE-2.1E energy model.

Review verification reports and suspicious inputs before running alternatives. A baseline built from incorrect schedules or equipment assumptions cannot support reliable investment comparisons.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Alternative design questions

Compare insulation, glazing, window location and area, lighting density, daylighting, transformer sizing and variable-speed/high-efficiency technologies.

For mechanical systems, compare zoning, plant capacity and unit count, HVAC type, chiller/boiler/pump specifications, thermal storage and solar-thermal alternatives.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Life-cycle investment assessment

Evaluate construction cost and annual energy cost together. A large modeled energy reduction does not necessarily produce the most favorable retrofit investment.

The source describes a consulting and decision framework; it does not provide a confirmed project-wide savings percentage or completed investment outcome.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Verification after implementation

Retain the assumptions and selected alternative as the design record. Compare actual post-retrofit utilities with a baseline adjusted for weather, occupancy and operating changes.

The practical value is a defensible sequence from physical condition through model quality to investment choice.

Evidence & interpretation

This case presents an engineering consulting framework. No quantified achieved saving or universal retrofit optimum is asserted.

Source basis: Existing-building equipment-condition assessment and retrofit simulation consulting material.
