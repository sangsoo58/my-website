CASE67 · Manufacturing · Boiler combustion correlations

Using stack temperature and oxygen to investigate boiler fuel-intensity changes

Field correlations connect gas-per-steam intensity to flue-gas temperature and oxygen. They guide inspection and combustion tests while preserving the distinction between observed association and confirmed cause.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Move from poor efficiency to an explainable question

Fuel intensity can worsen because of load, combustion, heat transfer, feedwater or metering. An intensity alarm alone does not identify the correct action.

The source separately analyzed final stack temperature and flue-gas O₂ against boiler intensity.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

What the field analysis showed

The material reported more favorable efficiency at lower observed stack temperature and lower observed O₂. Individual-boiler correlations also included load and outdoor conditions.

These are field associations without a published independent saving from adjusting only the two variables.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Interpret combustion within its constraints

Excess air increases the amount of heated exhaust, while too little air can produce incomplete combustion. Lower O₂ is not an unconditional operating objective.

Use qualified combustion checks including CO/combustibles, flame stability and burner conditions when testing a change. The source supplies no universal safe O₂ setpoint.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Differentiate possible causes

Persistently high stack temperature can motivate checks of heat-transfer surfaces, economizer, firing rate, air supply and sensor condition. Persistently high O₂ motivates checking air/fuel control and sensor calibration.

These are engineering diagnostic directions, not confirmed source findings that all these faults occurred.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Test and verify

Align gas, steam, load, pressure and flue-gas measurements. Compare similar load regions, make a controlled change and remeasure specific fuel use.

No savings percentage or annual amount is added where the source provides only correlation and optimization direction.

Evidence & interpretation

The source documents correlations and an optimization direction, not an isolated final saving. No universal O₂ or stack-temperature target is inferred.

Source basis: Boiler operating-characteristic correlation analysis and monitoring plan.
