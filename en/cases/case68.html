CASE68 · Manufacturing · Steam-meter data quality

Validating steam measurement before ranking boiler efficiency or claiming savings

Abnormal steam-flow values compromise the denominator of gas-per-steam intensity. The case prioritizes calibration and physical consistency before quantitative savings evaluation.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

The denominator can reverse the ranking

Specific fuel use is gas in Nm³ divided by steam production in tonnes. Under-reported steam makes intensity look worse; over-reported steam can create false apparent efficiency.

A sensor being installed does not establish that its stored data are valid for analysis.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Source finding and calculation hold

Normally metered boilers were reported around 43.9–49.6 Nm³/tonne. Some steam-flow measurements were abnormal, and the source required calibration before calculating quantitative savings.

The intensity range is not a saving rate, and abnormal units are not used to manufacture a benefit.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Validate the complete measurement chain

Check signal ranges, zero/span, communications, units, timestamps and compensation logic against raw instruments and calibration records.

Compare fuel, firing/load and steam patterns; use system mass/energy consistency to flag implausible values. A calculated soft-sensor estimate is a diagnostic aid, not automatically a replacement certified measurement.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Meter principles and steam state

DP/orifice and vortex systems infer flow through their principles and may require pressure/temperature/density compensation. Coriolis directly measures mass but feasibility still depends on the steam application.

Choose by range, pressure loss, installation, process state and required M&V confidence. This edition does not supply detailed standard compliance rules or a universal best meter.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data-quality gate before performance claims

Tag missing, frozen, spiking and biased intervals; distinguish actual startup losses from sensor faults. Preserve raw values and document exclusions and corrections.

Resume quantitative performance evaluation only after the measurement basis and applicable uncertainty are supported.

Evidence & interpretation

43.9–49.6 Nm³/tonne describes normally metered source units. Calibration was a prerequisite for savings calculation in abnormal units; no quantified saving is asserted.
