CASE32 · Buildings · 15-minute electricity analytics

Revealing building load structure from 35,136 fifteen-minute measurements

A twelve-month metered series is reorganized by month, day type and hour to investigate night loads, winter HVAC and peak-demand priorities. Estimated end uses remain distinct from measured whole-building electricity.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Why interval data matter

Monthly bills cannot identify which hours contain persistent night loads, holiday operation, early HVAC starts or peak events. Each day was reconstructed as a 96-point load profile.

The source describes the period as one year. It spans 366 calendar days because February 2024 is a leap month; 366 × 96 agrees with the reported 35,136 points.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Six-step analysis

Check interval data quality; build daily profiles; classify Monday, other weekdays, Saturday and holidays; analyze representative hourly patterns; estimate base, lighting/plug and HVAC components; rank operating opportunities.

Annual energy, average demand and maximum demand come from the actual whole-building meter. End-use segregation is a pattern-based estimate, not submetering.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Measured operating patterns

Average demand was 302.3 kW. Maximum demand reached 768.2 kW at 10:30 on 9 January 2024; the annual 97.5th percentile was 525.6 kW.

January electricity was approximately 340.6 MWh, while June and September were each approximately 149.6 MWh. Weekday mean demand was around 273 kW during 00:00–05:00 and 319 kW during 09:00–17:00.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Estimated load structure and priorities

Estimated annual shares were 61.3% base load, 35.8% HVAC and 2.9% lighting/plug loads. These are analytical allocations with uncertainty, not independently measured end uses.

Review essential versus avoidable night loads, winter HVAC start/stop and setpoints, and sequential starting or demand control during the highest-load intervals.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Interpretation and M&V

High night load is not automatically waste in a continuously operated facility. Confirm building use, occupancy and required service before changing schedules.

Verify implemented measures using adjusted baselines and actual HVAC/operating records. The diagnosis does not report an achieved savings percentage.
