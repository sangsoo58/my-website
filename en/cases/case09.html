CASE09 · Manufacturing · Chiller systems

Building monthly chiller operating plans from COP and seasonal demand

Hourly operating records support efficient-unit priority, monthly staging and chilled-water setpoints for 5°C and 12°C systems. Pump conditions are standardized so that equipment performance can be compared fairly.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Outside the summer peak, the plant had operating capacity above the calculated requirement. Chillers of the same capacity showed different COPs, making performance-based sequencing preferable to an unexamined rotation schedule.

Different chilled-water outlet temperatures and pump flows made direct equipment comparison difficult. Cleanroom dehumidification during humid weather also limited how far water temperatures could be raised.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and comparison method

The analysis combined equipment specifications, hourly COP, monthly required and actual unit counts, outlet temperatures, pump power and water flows for the 5°C and 12°C systems.

COP is cooling output divided by chiller electrical input. The report proposes standardizing chilled-water flow to an AHRI-equivalent reference of 545 m³/h for a 1,000 RT machine, with cooling-water conditions also aligned between running units. This is the source report’s comparison reference, not a substitute for equipment-specific limits.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Monthly operating strategy

Select higher-COP units first, add capacity according to required load and use seasonal setpoints. The 5°C system plan ranges from roughly 2–3 units in low-load periods to a maximum range of 6–12 in peak summer periods; the 12°C system uses roughly 2–3 and 5–6, respectively.

For the 5°C system, the proposed outlet range is about 5.5–7.0°C by season; the 12°C system uses approximately 12°C. Humid-season cleanroom requirements take priority.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Evidence and verification

The source gives a general operating rule that a 1°C increase in outlet setpoint may reduce chiller energy by approximately 2%. That rule is not a measured saving for this project.

The report does not provide confirmed annual savings after implementation. Validate the plan using cooling load, total cooling-system electricity, weather, production and cleanroom conditions.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

Staging, setpoints and pump conditions should be coordinated. Comparing chiller COP without comparable water and load conditions can produce misleading operating priorities.

Evidence & interpretation

Unit ranges describe the source monthly plan, not universal instructions. This case documents an operating scenario and contains no confirmed post-implementation annual saving.

Source basis: 2019 monthly chiller operating-plan report.
