CASE23 · Manufacturing · Smelting fumers

Combining feed allocation and fuel/oxygen control in zinc smelting

Two fumers differ in feed mix and energy intensity. A proposal combines slag/feed redistribution with real-time flue-gas O₂/CO feedback to reduce excessive fuel and oxygen input.

Smelting fumers

Coal, oxygen and electricity

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Coal and oxygen costs were large, and the two fumers operated with different feed mixtures and intensities. Changing feed properties affected combustion while existing utility controls could not always respond promptly.

Flue-gas O₂ and CO varied over time and between units, indicating a need to distinguish incomplete-combustion losses from excessive oxygen input.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and correlations

The analysis combined feed composition/input, throughput, coal/oxygen consumption, quality, equipment condition and flue-gas records. Higher slag proportion was associated with lower energy intensity.

One fumer’s seasonal intensity variation reached approximately 40.2%, compared with 4.4% for the other. Coal demand was examined against throughput, and oxygen against fuel input and combustion state. These are operating relationships, not standalone savings results.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Integrated proposal

Redistribute slag/feed mix toward approximately 30% while maintaining total throughput. The source simulation estimates approximately KRW 2.8 billion/year but does not separately account for slag transport cost.

Add O₂/CO analyzers and PLC-based closed-loop control for the two units. Fuel and oxygen setpoints are adjusted according to combustion condition to limit both unburned fuel and excess high-cost oxygen.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Expected economics and verification

The flue-gas-control proposal assumes approximately 1.8% coal and 7% oxygen savings, worth around KRW 500 million/year. Investment is approximately KRW 800 million with a source-reported payback of about 1.8 years.

The combined approximately KRW 3.3 billion/year is a consulting potential. Verify using feed composition/throughput, transport conditions, coal/oxygen prices, O₂/CO, product quality and utilization.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

Feed mass balance and combustion control interact. Preserve product and environmental requirements while evaluating the integrated proposal, and include omitted transport costs before using the historical economics for a new investment.

Evidence & interpretation

The financial amounts are proposal-stage estimates. The stated payback follows historical report assumptions and is not recalculated from the rounded displayed annual saving.

Source basis: 2009 zinc-smelting fumer energy-saving consulting completion report.
