CASE61 · Manufacturing · Compressed-air demand analysis

Reducing demand variability before deciding the compressor count

Demand peaks are traced upstream to production activity, storage and control behavior. The plan first changes the demand profile, then reassesses required online capacity.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Is a start caused by sustained demand or a short peak?

The source reported large load swings that led to standby capacity or extra compressor operation. Its improvement idea was demand analysis, smaller usage swings and minimum necessary running units.

The causal sequence starts at demand rather than arbitrarily switching off supply machines.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Historical context and event profiling

Annual analysis reported one or two idling machines and roughly two extra units at approximately 74% average load. Separate improvement records described two continuously unloaded machines totaling 440 kW despite a stated 88% load condition.

These are different source observations, not one matched test or a 440 kW saving attributable to this activity.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Overlay production, flow and control

Build daily/weekly profiles with mean, upper demand levels, peak duration and rate of change. Overlay header pressure, machine starts and loading/unloading with production shifts, startups and intermittent uses.

Short peaks may be buffered by appropriate storage or staggered usage; long sustained peaks may require genuinely higher capacity.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Reassess capacity after demand improvement

Investigate simultaneous starts, intermittent blowing, local actuators, leaks and dryer purge as possible causes. These are diagnostic candidates, not confirmed causes in the source.

After a feasible change, remeasure the profile and choose dispatch while preserving terminal pressure and required service.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Verify total system response

Compare delivered air, total compressor electricity, loading/unloading, pressure stability and production output. Avoid calculating savings from the nameplate power of an unused machine.

No isolated final benefit is published for this demand-variability plan. Any future predictive sequencing remains an extension requiring site data.

Evidence & interpretation

The 440 kW and load observations are contextual records, not a standalone saving for CASE61. No assumed demand cause or independent realized savings rate is invented.

Source basis: CDA load-variability diagnosis and demand-based minimum-unit optimization plan.
