CASE33 · Buildings · BEMS/BPOS diagnostic design

Designing a BEMS management model around capacity, operating time and efficiency

An end-use hierarchy links building demand and supply to equipment, management variables and operating guidance, turning consumption displays into a repeatable diagnostic process.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Management objective

Separate heating, cooling, ventilation, lighting, plug loads, hot water, cooking/process and vertical transportation. Map electricity and fuel/heat supply to these demands.

The model is intended to explain why energy is used and which variables operators can manage.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Diagnostic hierarchy

Classify end uses, map consuming equipment, define capacity/time/equipment-efficiency/operating-efficiency variables, analyze actual states and maintain improvement actions.

The capacity–time–efficiency breakdown is an engineering organizing model, not a calibrated statistical baseline with a published fit score.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Equipment-level management

Heating and cooling require pump and plant schedules, equipment performance and dispatch efficiency. Ventilation, lighting and plug loads require operating-time and efficiency management.

Elevators, hot-water equipment and process/support machines additionally require explicit capacity review.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

From monitoring to action

Use the hierarchy to define BEMS/BPOS menus, meter points, trends and operating alerts. Investigate unnecessary operation, excessive service and low efficiency.

The source does not state a measured project-wide savings rate; the outcome is a diagnosis and management design.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Verification and transfer

Define measurement boundaries and normalize for weather, occupancy and service demand when measures are implemented. Compare actual use with the adjusted baseline.

A dashboard becomes useful when each indicator has an equipment owner, a diagnostic question and an appropriate operating action.

Evidence & interpretation

This case documents a diagnostic/management framework, not quantified verified savings or a completed statistical model.
