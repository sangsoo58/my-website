CASE25 · Manufacturing · Compressed air

Optimizing compressor dispatch and loading/unloading with ten-second data

A graphite and battery-materials factory uses short-interval power, flow and pressure measurements to evaluate large base-load compressors and smaller demand-following units. The proposal combines essential metering with real-time operating guidance.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Rapidly recurring demand changes caused smaller compressors to switch frequently between loading and unloading. Large machines supplied the base load, making dispatch across different capacities a central operating question.

The system operated reasonably well overall. The aim was to sustain efficient combinations and distinguish equipment deterioration from inefficient system operation.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Measurements and performance indicators

Power, discharge pressure, header flow, active-unit count and unloading states were aligned at ten-second intervals. System UPI is power in kW divided by airflow in m³/h, giving kWh/m³.

Eight compressors were included in the proposed scope, including two planned additions. The minimum instrumentation package comprised eight current points, ten pressure points and two flow points.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating recommendations

Use measured efficiency and demand to choose large base-load and smaller regulating units. Review pressure bands and control transitions when frequent unloading is observed.

An average unloading loss of only 0.3% should not obscure short events reaching approximately 15%. Examine the associated demand and control states rather than treating the peak as an annual savings rate.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Proposal and economics

Annual electricity was approximately 8,741,345 kWh, with a historical electricity cost near KRW 874 million. The proposal estimated approximately 4%, or KRW 35 million/year plus unquantified additional benefits.

The proposal separated consulting/training, metering/server procurement and software deployment. Six months of consulting and six months of system construction were envisaged.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Verification and transfer

Establish calibrated flow and pressure measurements before setting the operating baseline. Verify electricity under comparable delivered-air volume, pressure, production demand and operating hours.

Reduced power accompanied by reduced service pressure or airflow is not automatically an efficiency improvement.

Evidence & interpretation

The 4% and KRW 35 million/year figures are proposal estimates, not verified post-implementation savings. The “plus” benefit is not quantified.

Source basis: 2018 compressor measurements and operating-optimization system proposal.
