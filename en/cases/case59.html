CASE59 · Manufacturing · Compressor efficiency degradation

Separating compressor equipment deterioration from operating-condition effects

Unit-level specific energy identifies persistently inefficient candidates. Matched pressure and load comparisons precede mechanical inspection, maintenance and remeasurement.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Compare energy with air output

Specific energy = compressor power in kW / airflow in Nm³/h, yielding kWh/Nm³. High kW alone does not establish poor efficiency if a machine delivers more air.

The source listed inspection of inefficient units and diagnosis/restoration of deterioration separately from header-pressure control and staging.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

System context

Annual analysis found one or two idling machines through the seasons, pressures around 8.3–9.0 kg/cm² and roughly two units operating above demand at around 74% mean load.

Source air-production yield was approximately 7.0 m³/kWh versus a stated theoretical reference of 8.0–8.3 m³/kWh. Higher yield is favorable; it is reciprocal to energy-per-air-volume intensity.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Do not diagnose mechanical failure from an unmatched KPI

Higher discharge pressure or frequent unloading can worsen specific energy even in a mechanically healthy compressor. Compare similar flow, pressure and load-factor regions.

Only persistent deviations after operating-condition checks justify detailed equipment inspection.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Inspect causes and verify restoration

Review intake filters/conditions, compression elements and internal leaks, lubrication, cooling, discharge valves, control settings and meter validity.

After action, remeasure under comparable conditions. A completed maintenance work order does not itself demonstrate restored energy performance.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Manage long-term drift

Build unit-specific normal ranges or load/pressure curves and track sustained deviations. Retain post-maintenance performance history.

The case does not publish an isolated final annual savings amount for the inspection activity.

Evidence & interpretation

System observations are diagnostic context, not proof that one compressor failed or that the benchmark gap was saved. Theoretical yield is not substituted for field M&V.

Source basis: CDA operating analysis and low-efficiency-unit diagnosis/restoration activity.
