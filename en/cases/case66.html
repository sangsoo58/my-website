CASE66 · Manufacturing · Boiler load and capacity combinations

Comparing boiler capacity combinations to avoid inefficient low-load operation

Field analysis identifies increasing specific fuel consumption below a source load range. Tests compare a single larger boiler with two smaller units while dispatch criteria remain tied to steam service.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Lower fuel use is not necessarily better intensity

A boiler can consume less total gas while requiring more gas per tonne of steam at low output. Fixed, standby or cycling losses may become more significant.

The source observed increased intensity below approximately 40% load. This is a project observation, not every boiler’s turndown or cycling limit.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Recorded capacity tests

The source sequence used unit #2 at 30 tonnes/hour up to 20 October, unit #3 at 30 tonnes/hour during 20–21 October, then units #1 and #5 at 15 tonnes/hour each during 21–23 October.

The capacity table documents test combinations; it does not provide the missing paired intensity results needed to select a universally superior combination.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Define load and fuel at the system level

Relate actual steam output to the sum of online rated capacity. Record gas, steam, pressure, unit availability and burner behavior on the same time axis.

Compare matched steam-demand regions and stable conditions. Two smaller boilers have the same combined rated capacity here but different individual operating characteristics.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Build feasible dispatch bands

Consider unit efficiency curves, minimum firing, start/stop behavior, pressure control and reserve. Add adequate hysteresis/runtime conditions when implementing staging.

A modern condensing hot-water boiler can have different part-load behavior from the industrial steam boilers described by this case.
