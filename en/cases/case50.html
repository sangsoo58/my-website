Comparing window area, glazing shading coefficient and curtains in an office model

The same building model isolates three fenestration changes. Their annual energy-cost impacts are interpreted with thermal transfer, solar gain and daylighting kept distinct.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Fenestration is more than facade area

Windows affect heat transfer, solar gain and useful daylight. Window-to-wall ratio, U-value, shading coefficient and visible transmission answer different performance questions.

The source changes one fenestration condition at a time; it does not identify a universal optimum window ratio.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Window-area comparison

Reducing the window ratio from 63% to 40% changed annual cost from approximately KRW 3,114 million to KRW 3,060 million. The reported reduction was KRW 54 million/year, or 1.7%.

Electricity and gas cost fell by approximately KRW 47 million and KRW 7 million/year respectively.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Glazing and curtain comparisons

Changing SC from 0.33 to 0.75 increased modeled annual cost to KRW 3,405 million: KRW 291 million/year or 9.3%. Electricity cost rose 7.8% and gas cost 32.5%.

Applying curtains at the source’s 50% condition reduced cost to KRW 3,083 million: KRW 31 million/year or 1.0%. Curtain material and use schedules affect transferability.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Interpret the result without inventing a cause

Higher SC can increase solar heat gain, but the full electricity/gas response depends on hourly heat balance and HVAC. The source result does not prove one sole causal mechanism.

Treat U-value, solar control and visible transmission separately. Evaluate orientation, glare, comfort, daylight controls and construction cost when choosing a real facade.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Compare complete alternatives

Combine feasible glazing, window area, shading and lighting-control choices in a fresh model run. Do not add these single-case percentages.

CASE49 and CASE50 share the virtual-office scenario study. Their values are related design comparisons rather than independent realized savings projects.

Evidence & interpretation

The source reports simulations, not measured retrofit savings. WWR 40% is a scenario input, not a universal optimum. SC values are preserved rather than silently converted to SHGC.

Source basis: Virtual-office window/glazing/curtain simulation comparisons.
