CASE27 · Water & wastewater · Water-supply pumps

Integrating pump efficiency, reservoir levels and peak-demand control

Seasonal ten-minute data across intake, treated-water transmission and booster systems support efficient pump selection, demand-based staging and reservoir scheduling.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Large pumps were distributed across several water-supply systems. Some individual flow and power values were unavailable directly, requiring calculated power and system-level information.

Reservoir levels, required head and peak electricity demand had to be reviewed together while maintaining reliable water service.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Diagnostic method

Nine major pump systems were compared using seasonal power, flow, pressure, load ratio and actual versus required active-unit counts.

The source defines pump UPI as delivered volume divided by electricity, in m³/kWh. Higher values are favorable. This is the reciprocal of the energy-per-output convention used in many other cases.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating strategy

Prioritize pumps with better measured efficiency at the applicable operating point. Check the demand-based number of running pumps against actual dispatch.

The proposed reservoir strategy combines lower night-time levels to reduce head with sufficient stored water before peak periods. Storage may allow temporary pump shutdown during the electrical peak, subject to service constraints.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Proposed savings and project scope

The 35-pump portfolio used approximately 27,155 MWh/year and KRW 1.97 billion/year in electricity. The reported total expected benefit was KRW 62 million/year, approximately 3%.

A nine-month project was proposed: six months of construction and three months of optimization, with an estimated investment of KRW 248 million.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

M&V and engineering lesson

Separate consumption savings from demand-charge savings. Adjust comparisons for flow, head, reservoir level, tariffs and demand conditions.

Reservoir scheduling is a constrained service decision: apparent electrical savings must preserve water availability and required pressure.

Evidence & interpretation

Seasonal diagnosis and proposal economics are not confirmed implementation results. The three cost components must retain their energy/demand-charge boundaries.

Source basis: Water-supply pump operating analysis and energy-efficiency management system proposal.
