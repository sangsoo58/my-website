CASE22 · Manufacturing · Blast-furnace blowers

Diagnosing blast-furnace blower intensity, pressure and IGV operation

Seasonal blower data separate equipment differences from weather effects. Pressure margins, duct losses and unusual IGV readings are investigated while avoiding an unsupported filter-efficiency claim.

English technical edition · Original case and supporting material (Korean) ↗
Field diagnosis

Reported annual blower intensity range

0.075–0.081 kWh/Nm³
Site
Anonymized integrated steelworks B, Korea
Equipment
Blast-furnace blowers
Analysis period
January, April, July and October 2018; longer-term intake-filter data
Energy
Electricity

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Annual blower electricity cost was approximately KRW 145 billion. Seasonal airflow changed little, but summer power and intensity increased.

Some blowers showed very low or negative IGV readings despite high load, calling for field checks of signal consistency and control logic. Intake-filter differential pressure did not show a clear correlation with intensity.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and diagnostic indicators

The comparison used electricity/cost, power, normalized airflow, intensity, load, IGV opening, discharge/furnace-side pressures, duct losses, weather and intake-filter ΔP.

Overall intensity averaged approximately 0.078 kWh/Nm³; unit values ranged from about 0.075 to 0.081. Average power was 33.1 MW per furnace, about 34.8 MW in summer. Summer intensity was approximately 7.2% above winter.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating opportunities

Compare efficient blower conditions under the required blast flow and pressure. Review unnecessary pressure margins only while satisfying furnace operating and safety requirements.

The reported mean supply pressure was approximately 4.3 kg/cm², with some furnaces at 4.5 against a lower group around 4.0. Duct loss averaged 0.13 kg/cm² and reached about 0.09 in the lowest-loss furnace. Verify unusual IGV signals before changing control.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Evidence and M&V

The maximum approximately 8.2% unit-intensity spread is not a savings rate. The report does not provide confirmed post-improvement energy or cost savings.

Use production conditions, required blast flow/pressure, ambient temperature and blower combinations in the baseline. A lack of clear filter-ΔP correlation means filter work should not be assigned a quantified benefit without further evidence.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

Diagnosis includes findings that do not support the initial hypothesis. Record the absence of clear filter correlation and prioritize the better-supported pressure, signal and operating-point investigations.

Evidence & interpretation

Performance spreads and seasonal differences are diagnostic observations. They cannot be converted directly into annual savings. Pressure units and rounded differences follow the Korean source.

Source basis: 2018 integrated steelworks blast-furnace blower operating analysis.
