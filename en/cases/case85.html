CASE85 · Buildings · Central boiler / HDD schedule

Using heating degree days to guide central-boiler operating hours

A historical-weather model replaces stepped minimum-temperature schedules with an HDD-based daily runtime guide. The result is an operating-hour potential, not a measured fuel saving.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Define the model and heating season

The source uses 2023 Daejeon hourly temperatures and January–March plus November–December heating operation. Existing starts were 04:30, 09:00, 16:00 and 21:00.

The source HDD expression is 18 − (daily maximum + daily minimum temperature) / 2. The guide uses this temperature-based demand index to determine total daily boiler hours.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Compare the existing and proposed guides

Existing control assigned each of four runs a duration from daily minimum temperature: 0, 0.50, 1.00, 1.50, 1.67 or 1.83 hours.

The proposed guide uses 0 hours below HDD 5, 1 hour from 5 to below 9, and 2 hours from 9 to below 12. At HDD 12 it moves to 4.0 hours, increasing linearly to 7.3 hours at HDD 30 with a 7.3-hour cap.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Interpret the simulated annual result

Reported existing runtime was 843.8 h/year and proposed runtime 528.9 h/year, a difference of 314.9 h/year or 37.32%.

These are simulation results for operating hours. Boiler capacity, part-load efficiency and metered fuel are not provided to justify an identical gas-saving percentage.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Calibrate and verify at the site

Allocate total daily hours across the four operating periods and check room temperature, supply/return temperatures, complaints and actual gas. Trial mild conditions before widening the operating range.

Separate space heating from domestic-hot-water demand when meters combine them. Actual fuel savings require a weather-adjusted baseline and measured reporting consumption.
