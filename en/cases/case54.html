CASE54 · Manufacturing · Header-pressure control

Moving compressor pressure control from individual outlets to the common header

Common-header and critical end-use pressure measurements support integrated sequencing and staged pressure tests. The target is adequate process service with minimum system energy.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Choose the service-side control reference

Independent compressor discharge controls can interact without accurately representing production requirements. The source action changed the pressure sensing/control reference from individual compressors toward the common CDA header.

The starting point is the minimum pressure needed at the critical process use, not the compressor’s rated pressure.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Measure the pressure network

Align compressor discharge, header, branch and terminal pressure with airflow, unit power and loading/unloading.

Required header pressure is approximately critical end-use minimum pressure + distribution pressure drop + necessary operating margin. Investigate filters, dryers, piping and valves before using higher pressure to conceal losses.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Coordinate pressure and staging

Common pressure control and compressor dispatch are one system problem. Narrower stable pressure operation may reduce unnecessary loading/unloading while maintaining short-term demand response.

Do not transfer an absolute setpoint from another factory: terminal requirements, air quality and demand patterns differ.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Test in stages

Establish baseline service and total power, then adjust the setpoint gradually and wait for stabilization. Confirm the critical terminal pressure and process operation at each stage.

The source lists approximately KRW 230 million/year as an expected benefit for an item still in progress, not a completed independently measured saving.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

M&V boundary

Compare total compressor electricity and specific energy under matched delivered-air volume, pressure requirements and production conditions. Keep dispatch, storage and distribution changes documented.

CASE52 concerns running-unit count; CASE54 concerns network pressure. Their interaction does not justify adding unrelated scenario savings.

Evidence & interpretation

KRW 230 million/year is improvement potential for an in-progress action. No final pressure reduction, measured saving rate or independent realized annual benefit is invented.

Source basis: CDA header-pressure sensing/control improvement item and project plan.
