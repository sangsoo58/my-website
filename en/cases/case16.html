CASE16 · Manufacturing · Compressed air

Optimizing automotive compressed air through UPI, staging and demand patterns

Power, airflow, pressure, IGV/BOV behavior and process-level demand are analyzed together. A small UPI improvement represents a meaningful cost opportunity in a large compressed-air system.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Operating challenge

Annual compressor electricity was approximately 37.12 million kWh. Separate high- and low-pressure circuits required demand-based staging and pressure review.

IGV control was generally satisfactory, but BOV opening during supply-demand imbalance was associated with higher losses and UPI. Three major production processes accounted for about 73% of air demand.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Data and performance diagnosis

The analysis combined system power, normalized airflow, supply/discharge pressure, running/required units, IGV opening, BOV state, load, loss and process-level flow patterns.

Mean UPI was approximately 0.106 kWh/Nm³ against a proposed reference of 0.104. Average load was about 91%. The detailed actual-versus-required table reports about 4.6 operating units against 4.1–4.2 required, within a 2–8-unit operating range.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Supply and demand measures

Select efficient unit combinations according to airflow demand, review pressure margins against end-use needs and pressure losses, and reduce BOV venting through better supply-demand balancing.

On the demand side, prioritize the main consuming processes and examine peaks, non-production periods and abnormal usage using their flow meters. Existing pressure control was generally sound, so adjustments should be incremental and protect production requirements.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Potential and M&V

The source estimates KRW 70,041,685/year if the mean UPI approaches the 0.104 reference. This is an operating-data-based cost potential.

Verify with air production, production conditions, operating hours, pressure and demand reflected in the baseline. BOV loss correlation supports diagnosis but does not alone quantify a verified saving.

05 · CASE ANALYSIS

Engineering lesson

At large utility scale, small intensity changes can matter. Supply optimization and demand management should be coordinated, with the measured airflow denominator kept consistent.
