CASE72 · Data centers · Economizer / Free cooling

Screening outdoor-air cooling alternatives for data centers

Climate, humidity, air quality and cooling architecture determine whether free cooling is useful. The diagnosis compares direct-air, indirect-air and water-side options at the system boundary.

01 · CASE ANALYSIS

Outdoor temperature alone is insufficient

The source reviews outdoor-air cooling as an alternative in data-center improvement work. Dry-bulb temperature is only one screening variable.

Include humidity, dew point, air quality, IT load, required supply conditions and reliability. Suitable outdoor conditions do not guarantee that the existing plant can use them.

02 · CASE ANALYSIS

Compare three architectures

Direct-air systems introduce conditioned outdoor air. Indirect-air systems exchange heat while keeping outdoor air separated. Water-side economizers reduce or bypass mechanical refrigeration through a water loop.

Each architecture has different fan, pump, heat-exchanger, filtration, humidity-control and changeover requirements.

03 · CASE ANALYSIS

Estimate useful annual hours

Use weather bins and actual IT-load profiles to identify feasible operating hours. Include the electricity needed by fans and pumps and any humidity-treatment penalty.

Cool dry conditions may require humidification and filtration; hot dry conditions raise water and evaporative-treatment questions; humid conditions make dew-point control essential.

04 · CASE ANALYSIS

Implement a bounded operating trial

Verify changeover stability, rack conditions, sensor quality and local fallback before extending operation. Evaluate total cooling electricity rather than compressor power alone.

The source supports feasibility and economic review; no separate final saving amount for CASE72 is established.
